HERO REVISION — “THINGS” AS LIVING OBJECT TYPOGRAPHY

Use the two newly attached visual references as the primary inspiration for this revision.

IMPORTANT: This is a fundamental change to the hero collection concept.

Do not create a pile of objects that sits beside the typography.

Do not scatter decorative objects around the hero.

Instead:

THE WORD “THINGS” ITSELF SHOULD BE MADE FROM MANY SMALL “THINGS”.

The hero should read:

I FIGURE
THINGS
OUT.

But:

“I FIGURE” and “OUT.” are clean, stable, large typography.

“THINGS” is a dynamic composition made from many small physical objects, fragments, marks, textures and visual things.

The objects collectively form the readable silhouette of the word THINGS.

--------------------------------------------------
CORE VISUAL IDEA
--------------------------------------------------

The phrase is:

I FIGURE THINGS OUT.

The word THINGS is literally composed of things.

The viewer should initially read:

I FIGURE
THINGS
OUT.

Then notice that THINGS is not normal typography.

It is made from many small objects.

The objects continuously reorganize themselves while preserving the overall silhouette of the word.

The experience should feel intelligent, curious, tactile and slightly strange.

--------------------------------------------------
REFERENCE INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------

Use the attached references specifically for:

1. A large field of messy/random visual marks that can collectively form a composition.

2. Small individual fragments/objects collectively creating readable typography.

The references demonstrate the IDEA of:

MANY SMALL ELEMENTS → ONE CLEAR FORM.

Do NOT copy their exact typography, colours, layout, objects or visual style.

Use the compositional principle only.

Keep the warm off-white background and minimalist editorial aesthetic.

Do not redesign the entire homepage.

--------------------------------------------------
TYPOGRAPHY
--------------------------------------------------

“I FIGURE” and “OUT.” should remain:

- large
- bold
- black
- crisp
- stable
- highly legible

The word “THINGS” should occupy approximately the same typographic area as the other two lines.

It should NOT simply have a normal text layer underneath the objects.

The objects ARE the typography.

--------------------------------------------------
THE “THINGS” OBJECT FIELD
--------------------------------------------------

Create a large collection of many small physical-looking elements.

Approximately 80–200 small elements depending on performance.

They can include:

- tiny book fragments
- colourful plastic objects
- rocks
- bangles
- beads
- paper fragments
- handwritten marks
- pencil marks
- tiny photographs
- stationery
- jewellery
- key/keychain fragments
- small mechanical pieces
- colourful everyday objects
- material textures
- tiny abstract found objects

Use a mixture of shapes, sizes, colours and textures.

The objects should NOT all be identical.

The field should feel like a collection of real, miscellaneous “things.”

Avoid generic abstract blobs.

Avoid UI icons.

Avoid emoji.

Avoid flat vector shapes.

Avoid uniform particles.

Avoid a typical particle-system aesthetic.

The elements should feel tactile and physical.

--------------------------------------------------
INITIAL STATE
--------------------------------------------------

At page load:

The word THINGS must already be readable.

Do NOT make the visitor wait for it to form.

The initial state should be approximately:

THINGS

formed from many small things.

The individual objects should be visible enough that a curious visitor realizes the typography is made from objects.

However, the word should still be immediately readable from normal viewing distance.

--------------------------------------------------
MULTIPLE TYPOGRAPHIC VARIATIONS
--------------------------------------------------

Create 3 distinct compositions of the word THINGS.

VARIATION 01:
Dense, slightly messy arrangement.

VARIATION 02:
More open arrangement with stronger visibility of individual objects.

VARIATION 03:
More playful asymmetric arrangement with different object orientations and scale relationships.

All three must clearly read as:

THINGS

Do not simply use the same word shape and randomly move individual objects.

Each variation should have a deliberately different visual composition.

The letters should remain recognizable.

--------------------------------------------------
CONTINUOUS MORPHING
--------------------------------------------------

The three variations should repeat continuously:

01 → 02 → 03 → 01

The animation should be slow and organic.

Each variation should remain visible for approximately 4–7 seconds.

The transition between variations should take approximately 1–2 seconds.

Objects should move individually into new positions.

Do NOT:

- explode the word
- dissolve into particles
- scatter everything across the screen
- rebuild the word from zero
- use dramatic particle effects

Instead, the objects should gently exchange positions, rotate, shift, and settle into the next typographic composition.

The viewer should feel that the word is alive and constantly reconsidering itself.

--------------------------------------------------
MOTION CHARACTER
--------------------------------------------------

Motion should feel:

- organic
- physical
- thoughtful
- slightly unpredictable
- tactile
- restrained

Some objects move more than others.

Some barely move.

Some rotate.

Some shift laterally.

Some move vertically.

Some overlap.

Some separate.

The animation should feel like many physical things finding a new arrangement.

Do not make it feel like a screensaver.

Do not use excessive easing/bouncing.

Do not use flashy particle animation.

--------------------------------------------------
OBJECT SCALE
--------------------------------------------------

Create clear hierarchy within THINGS.

Most elements should be small.

Some should be medium.

A few should be larger visual anchors.

Do not make every element the same size.

Do not make the letters look like they're composed of hundreds of identical dots.

The visual language should feel closer to:

COLLAGE + STILL LIFE + TYPOGRAPHY

than:

PARTICLE SYSTEM + DIGITAL EFFECT.

--------------------------------------------------
RELATIONSHIP TO THE REST OF THE HERO
--------------------------------------------------

“I FIGURE” and “OUT.” remain extremely clean.

Only “THINGS” is visually unstable.

This creates the contrast:

I FIGURE
THINGS
OUT.

The stable typography gives the eye clarity.

The changing “THINGS” introduces curiosity.

Do not add additional decorative objects around the hero unless they are necessary to support the THINGS composition.

The hero should therefore become much cleaner than the current version.

--------------------------------------------------
OPTIONAL CURSOR INTERACTION
--------------------------------------------------

The automatic morphing should happen without requiring interaction.

The visitor can simply look at it and scroll.

However, if the visitor moves their cursor over THINGS, introduce a subtle interaction.

Cursor indicator:

LOOK

The objects immediately around the cursor can:

- shift slightly
- become slightly larger
- reveal their individual form
- move with subtle physical response

The rest of the word remains stable.

The goal is to allow curious users to “look closer” without interrupting the animation.

Do not require clicking.

Do not create a game.

--------------------------------------------------
DISCOVERY
--------------------------------------------------

There should be no instruction such as:

“Find the objects.”

“Move your mouse.”

“Discover the collection.”

The user should discover the interaction naturally.

The website should remain immediately understandable as a portfolio.

--------------------------------------------------
IMPORTANT COMPOSITION RULE
--------------------------------------------------

The objects must NOT feel like they are sitting on top of a pre-existing word.

Do NOT:

create the word “THINGS”
then place objects over the letters.

Instead:

THE OBJECTS THEMSELVES MUST FORM THE LETTERS.

There should be no visible conventional text layer underneath.

The silhouette of THINGS emerges from the arrangement of objects.
