Design and prototype a highly art-directed personal portfolio website for Shanvi Bhadauriya, a product designer.

The website should feel personal, intelligent, visually confident, playful, and slightly unexpected, while remaining extremely easy to understand and navigate.

The central personality of the website is:

I figure things out.

Shanvi is interested in messy, ambiguous problems involving people, products, behaviour, culture, technology, and the systems around them. She enjoys noticing when something doesn't make sense, understanding the connections underneath it, and figuring out what part can be changed to make the larger experience work better.

However, do not repeatedly describe her as a “systems thinker.” The website should demonstrate this way of thinking through its work and interactions rather than announcing it as a title.

The portfolio should communicate:

curiosity → observation → figuring things out → thoughtful design

The viewer should leave thinking:

“She seems really good at figuring things out. I want to see what she can do.”

01 — OVERALL CREATIVE DIRECTION

Create a visual language that sits between:

editorial design × contemporary digital portfolio × art direction × playful interaction

The website should NOT feel like:

a generic Framer template
a standard UX portfolio
a corporate product designer portfolio
a design-school case-study website
a futuristic tech website
a WebGL showcase
a minimalist website with nothing interesting happening
an overly intellectual “systems thinking” website

Instead, aim for:

CLEAN FOUNDATION + STRONG ART DIRECTION + PERSONAL ODDITY

The interface itself should remain simple and comprehensible.

The visual content should provide the surprise.

Think:

Simple to understand. Interesting to explore.

The website should have controlled weirdness, not chaos.

02 — VISUAL PRINCIPLE

Use the principle:

The interface stays quiet. The idea gets loud.

Keep navigation, metadata, buttons, and supporting UI restrained.

Use visual impact through:

oversized typography
strong composition
art-directed imagery
unexpected physical objects
asymmetry
scale shifts
whitespace
subtle movement
occasional playful interactions

Avoid adding visual elements simply to make the site look “creative.”

Every unusual interaction should have a reason.

03 — THE PERSONAL COLLECTION

The signature visual element of the homepage is a personal collection of objects.

The collection should feel like a beautifully art-directed still life made from things Shanvi genuinely likes or keeps around.

The objects are:

Bhondu — a strange personal keychain with sentimental/personality value
A rock
My Year of Rest and Relaxation — the book
Colourful bangles
A handwritten note / piece of writing
A small representation of sound/music
Ashes

Do NOT make the objects into obvious metaphors.

Do NOT label the rock as “stability,” the ashes as “transformation,” etc.

They are simply things Shanvi likes.

Their randomness is part of the personality.

The collection should communicate:

“I pay attention to things.”

The objects should be rendered or photographed with a premium editorial still-life aesthetic.

Art direction for the collection:
clean neutral background
soft directional lighting
tactile materials
realistic physicality
interesting shadows
slightly surreal scale/composition
carefully controlled colour
generous negative space
no generic 3D blobs
no futuristic CGI aesthetic
no excessive gradients
no glossy startup aesthetic

The objects should look real enough to touch.

The overall collection should feel curated rather than randomly scattered.

Use 2–3 dominant objects and 3–4 smaller discoveries.

Bhondu should be visually recognisable and slightly playful.

05 — HERO COMPOSITION

Integrate the personal collection directly into the typography.

The objects should not sit inside cards.

They should occupy the same visual plane as the headline.

For example:

the book may be large and partially cropped
Bhondu may sit near or slightly overlap the typography
bangles can form a colourful circular composition
the rock can be tiny and almost hidden
handwriting can partially overlap the typography
ash can appear subtly as a texture
sound can be represented through a tiny visual element

Create depth and hierarchy, not clutter.

The composition should feel like an art-directed photograph interrupted by typography.

06 — HERO INTERACTIONS

Interactions must be subtle.

Cursor interaction

Objects respond gently to proximity.

Examples:

bangles shift against one another
the book moves a few pixels
the rock subtly rolls
handwriting becomes slightly more visible

Avoid exaggerated animation.

No objects flying around the screen.

No constant motion.

The website should still look beautiful when completely still.

Object hover

Some objects reveal small labels.

For example:

yes, that's his name.

The book:

MY YEAR OF REST AND RELAXATION

The writing:

The sound object:

Not every object needs an explanation.

Some objects can remain unexplained.

07 — SOUND

Include sound only as an optional interaction.

Never autoplay.

A small sound-related object can be clicked to play a very short audio fragment.

The sound interaction should be subtle and optional.

The page must work perfectly with sound disabled.

08 — HERO SCROLL TRANSITION

As the user scrolls past the hero, the personal collection should slowly disassemble.

Objects move toward different areas of the page.

The movement should feel physical and intentional.

The book, writing, rock, etc. gradually leave the hero.

The screen becomes quieter.

Then reveal:

SELECTED WORK

This transition establishes a conceptual relationship:

Things I keep around → things I work on

Do not explain this relationship with a paragraph.

Let the interaction communicate it.

12 — PROJECT STRUCTURE

Every project should feel like an investigation rather than a standard UX process.

Instead of rigidly presenting:

Problem → Research → Wireframes → UI → Conclusion

structure the narrative around:

I noticed something.

↓

I wondered why.

↓

I looked closer.

↓

I realised the problem was actually...

↓

So I changed...

↓

Here's what happened.

This should become a recurring storytelling pattern across case studies.

The exact wording can change per project.

Keep this elegant and fast.

Do not make users wait for animations.

Typography should be the main graphic device.

19 — GRID

Use a 12-column desktop grid.

Generous margins.

Allow intentional grid-breaking.

The site should feel structured underneath its playful visual layer.

Do not center everything.

Use asymmetric compositions where appropriate.

Maintain consistent alignment for navigation, metadata, and supporting information.

If removing an animation doesn't make the experience worse, remove it.

25 — MOST IMPORTANT DESIGN RULES

The final website must communicate:

1. She notices things.
2. She asks questions.
3. She figures things out.
4. She designs thoughtfully.

Do not literally repeat these four statements throughout the website.

Let the work and experience communicate them.

FINAL ART DIRECTION

The finished portfolio should feel like:

a very clean digital space containing a slightly strange collection of things belonging to a curious person.

It should have the confidence of a contemporary design studio site, the intimacy of a personal notebook, and the visual restraint of an editorial publication.

Not boring minimalism.

Not maximalist experimentation.

Clean, curious, tactile, art-directed, slightly weird.

The viewer should understand Shanvi's role immediately, be intrigued by the visual world, and then become increasingly interested in the way she approaches problems.

The website itself should make the viewer think:

“I don't know exactly what she's going to figure out next, but I want to see it.”
